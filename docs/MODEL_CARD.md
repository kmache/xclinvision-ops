# Model card — XClinVision chest X-ray classifiers

Covers the four checkpoints in `models/best_models/`: `vit_base`, `convnext_small`,
`efficientnet_b0`, `densenet`. Every number traces to
[`docs/model_evaluation.md`](model_evaluation.md), which is generated by executed code.

**These models are not for clinical use.** They are not a medical device, have no
regulatory clearance, and were never validated prospectively or on any patient
population outside the dataset below.

---

## Intended use

A research and portfolio artifact demonstrating an end-to-end medical-imaging ML system:
training, evaluation, calibration, explainability, retrieval-augmented reporting, and
serving. Appropriate uses:

- Studying how a multilabel chest X-ray classifier behaves end to end.
- Method development — calibration, thresholding, XAI, drift monitoring.
- A worked reference for building this class of system.

## Out-of-scope use

Not appropriate, in any configuration:

- **Any clinical decision affecting a patient** — triage, diagnosis, prioritisation,
  second reads, or screening.
- **Autonomous operation.** There is no mechanism to detect when the model is wrong.
- **Populations unlike the training data.** Trained on one Vietnamese hospital dataset;
  behaviour under other scanners, protocols, demographics, or paediatric imaging is
  unmeasured.
- **Findings outside the four classes below.** The model cannot report what it was never
  trained on, and its silence is not evidence of absence — a pneumothorax scores nothing
  because there is no pneumothorax head, not because there is no pneumothorax.
- **Regulatory submission or any claim of clinical validity.**

## Training data

| | |
|---|---|
| Source | VinBigData Chest X-ray Abnormalities Detection (VinDr-CXR), Kaggle |
| Size | 14,304 images after processing |
| Classes served | 4 findings, multilabel (`No finding` is filtered in multilabel mode) |
| Resolution | 384 × 384 |
| Label provenance | Radiologist annotations, 3 of 17 readers per image; majority vote over `rad_id` |
| Split | 10,020 train / 2,133 val / 2,151 test (70/15/15), iterative stratification |
| Split unit | **Image, not patient** — see leakage below |

**Licence: not recorded in this repository.** The only in-repo reference is `kagglehub`
in `requirements.txt`. VinDr-CXR is distributed under its own terms and the Kaggle
competition adds its own; verify both before redistributing data, weights, or derivatives.
Do not assume the permissive reading.

### Patient-level leakage check

- No image appears in more than one split: **0 of 14,304**.
- No byte-identical or near-identical pair across train/val. A perceptual-hash sweep
  raised 96 candidate collisions; all 40 sampled were **different images** (0 byte-identical,
  0 near-identical, median pixel MSE 2,281 vs 4,630 for random pairs). dHash has low
  entropy on chest radiographs, where every image shares anatomy and framing.
- **Patient identity cannot be verified.** VinBigData ships `image_id`, `class_name`,
  `class_id`, `rad_id` and bounding boxes — there is no patient identifier, so the split
  fell back to image level. If the source contains multiple studies of one patient they
  could straddle splits and no released field would reveal it. Nothing observed suggests
  it happened; it cannot be excluded.

### Pretraining

ImageNet, via `timm` (`pretrained: true` in all four configs). **No medical-domain
pretraining** anywhere in the pipeline.

## Calibration

**Status: calibrated**, for all four checkpoints.

Per class, `p = sigmoid(z / T + b)` — fitted by L-BFGS on per-class binary NLL over the
validation split, stored in the checkpoint payload as `temperature`, `calibration_bias`,
`calibration_status` and `calibration_ece`.

| | mean val ECE | mean test ECE |
|---|---|---|
| Before | 0.200 – 0.291 | 0.199 – 0.291 |
| After | 0.0134 – 0.0153 | **0.0147 – 0.0161** |

Why an affine map rather than plain temperature scaling: training used `pos_weight`
5.10–15.21, which biases every head toward positive. Temperature divides logits and cannot
shift them, so it cannot correct an offset — fitted alone it reaches only 0.17–0.28, and
three classes get worse. The fitted bias is negative on all 16 model×class cells, largest
where `pos_weight` was largest.

`calibration_status` flips to `calibrated` only when mean validation ECE improves **and**
lands at or below 0.05. A checkpoint failing that bar keeps its parameters, still applies
them, and continues reporting `uncalibrated` so the report layer keeps hedging.

**Caveat:** calibration was fitted on the same validation split used for model selection
and early stopping. There is no separate calibration split. Test ECE tracks validation to
~0.005, so the fit generalises, but the val figures are mildly optimistic.

## Limitations and failure modes

### 1. Two of the four classes are not individually usable

| Class | AUC | AP | Precision @ 90% recall | Verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Cardiomegaly | 0.957 | 0.757 | 0.57 | usable |
| Aortic enlargement | 0.959 | 0.809 | 0.61 | usable |
| Pleural thickening | 0.904 | 0.429 | 0.18 | **not usable** |
| Pulmonary fibrosis | 0.898 | 0.523 | 0.18 | **not usable** |

At 90% recall the two peripheral classes return roughly five to seven false positives per
true finding. Their ranking is real; their precision at any usable recall is not. This is
a two-finding system presented as a four-finding one, and calibration does not change it —
the limit is separability, not the number attached to it.

### 2. `horizontal_flip: true` contradicts the laterality claims in the XAI layer

`configs/system.yaml:9` enables horizontal flip augmentation during training, teaching the
model partial left/right invariance. `src/xclinvision/xai.py:49-58` then maps activation
regions by anatomical side ("Patient LEFT lung is on RIGHT of image") and the report
renders those side-specific labels as spatial evidence.

**Current resolution — measured, not assumed.** Evaluating flipped inputs costs at most
0.026 AUC (`densenet`, the two lower-zone classes) and typically under 0.005, so ranking
is nearly flip-invariant. But per-image probabilities move 0.03–0.09 mean absolute. The
model is therefore substantially insensitive to the very laterality the XAI layer asserts,
and a side-specific region label carries less information than its wording implies. The
flip remains enabled and the region labels remain side-specific; treat left/right
attribution in reports as unverified.

### 3. XAI faithfulness is entirely unvalidated

Grad-CAM++, Score-CAM and attention rollout are computed, displayed, and embedded in
exported clinical reports. **No faithfulness metric exists anywhere in the codebase** — no
deletion or insertion curves, no sanity checks, no localisation agreement against the
bounding boxes VinBigData ships. There is no evidence any heatmap reflects the model's
actual reasoning. A plausible-looking overlay on a wrong prediction is the expected
failure mode and nothing here would catch it.

### 4. Patient-level leakage cannot be excluded

See training data above. No duplicate imagery was found; patient identity is unverifiable
in the source, so every metric carries that caveat.

### 5. Authentication is a single shared token

`app/backend/auth.py` enforces one bearer token from `XCLINVISION_API_TOKEN`, failing
closed when unset. There is **no per-patient scoping, no RBAC, no per-user identity, and
no audit trail**. Any caller holding the token can read every stored analysis, image and
report. Adequate for a demo behind a firewall; not adequate for anything with real
patient data.

### 6. Other known gaps

- ImageNet pretraining, not medical-domain.
- Single dataset, single institution, single country. No external validation.
- No paediatric, portable/bedside, or lateral-view validation.
- No subgroup analysis by age or sex — the dataset ships neither.
- MC-Dropout uncertainty is reported but its calibration is unvalidated.
- Analyses and images persist unencrypted in SQLite and on the filesystem.

## Ethical considerations

**PHI posture.** The system stores uploaded images and derived analyses on the local
filesystem and in SQLite, unencrypted, with no de-identification step, no retention policy
beyond FIFO eviction, and no access logging. DICOM ingestion reads pixel data but does not
strip identifying tags. **Do not send real patient data to this system.** Treat any
deployment as a research sandbox with no PHI safeguards.

**Automation bias.** A confident probability beside a plausible heatmap invites trust the
evidence does not support — particularly acute here, where two classes are well below
usable precision and no heatmap has been validated for faithfulness. The report layer
hedges language for uncalibrated output; it does not and cannot flag a confidently wrong
prediction.

**Population representativeness.** One Vietnamese hospital dataset. Performance on other
populations, scanners and protocols is unmeasured, and unmeasured is not "probably fine" —
chest X-ray models are known to degrade across sites.

**Label ceiling.** Ground truth is a majority vote of three radiologists, not an outcome
or a tissue diagnosis. The model is trained to reproduce reader consensus, inheriting its
biases and its errors, and cannot exceed it.
